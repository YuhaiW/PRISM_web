Planning with physical and action intuition

In JEPA-based world models, predictive representations are typically used to evaluate what an action will do. PRISM measures how much action guidance can be extracted from them. It learns a goal-conditioned action prior from the frozen encoder's current and goal embeddings and integrates the prior into sampling-based model predictive control (MPC) through closed-form Product-of-Gaussians (PoG) fusion. It needs no additional visual encoder, pretrained policy, or language instructions, and accepts different action-head architectures.

Abstract

Latent world models learn representations that enable them to predict how actions change the environment—a capability we call physical intuition. We find that these same representations also support action intuition: a lightweight head can learn a goal-conditioned action prior directly from the frozen encoder's features. We introduce PRISM to investigate how action intuition can be extracted from frozen world-model representations and used to guide goal-conditioned planning. The prior's mean and predicted uncertainty guide the search for useful actions, while the world model evaluates their consequences against the goal. Experiments on four simulated control tasks and two real-robot platforms show that a lightweight action head improves planning performance, and that the same fusion interface accepts a diffusion prior over the same frozen features without modification. These results demonstrate the potential of reusing world-model representations to both predict action consequences and guide action selection.

Probe the frozen features

02

Learn a lightweight prior

A two-layer MLP maps current and goal embeddings to a diagonal Gaussian over the next action sequence, trained with β-NLL on cached features: 0.59M parameters on Cube, 0.51M on PushT. A diffusion head over the same features plugs into the same fusion interface.

03

Fuse by predicted uncertainty

A closed-form Product of Gaussians combines the prior with the planner's default proposal. As \(s\sigma_p\) grows, the fused proposal approaches the default. When training labels are corrupted, \(\sigma_p\) rises (0.53 → 4.49 on PushT) and success approaches prior-free planning.

Method

The encoder maps the current and goal observations to \(z_t = h_\psi(o_t)\) and \(z_g = h_\psi(o_g)\). The prior head predicts a diagonal Gaussian \((\mu_p, \sigma_p) = g_\phi([z_t, z_g])\) over \(H\) planning steps of \(B\) actions each (two 512-unit GELU layers, \(\sigma_p = \mathrm{softplus}(r_\phi) + 0.05\), β-NLL loss with β = 0.5). At every replanning step the prior is combined with the planner's default proposal \(\mathcal{N}(\mu_\pi, \mathrm{diag}(\sigma_\pi^2))\), \(\mu_\pi = 0\), by multiplying the two diagonal Gaussians per coordinate:

$$ \sigma^2_{\text{fused}} = (\tau_\pi + \tau_p)^{-1}, \qquad \mu_{\text{fused}} = \sigma^2_{\text{fused}}\,(\tau_\pi\,\mu_\pi + \tau_p\,\mu_p), $$ $$ \tau_\pi = \sigma_\pi^{-2}, \qquad \tau_p = (s\,\sigma_p)^{-2}. $$

The update depends only on \(s\sigma_p/\sigma_\pi\). All results use \(\sigma_\pi = 1.0\) in normalized action space, \(s = 1\) for the MLP head, and a floor of 0.05 on \(\sigma_{\text{fused}}\). MPPI then draws \(K\) candidates from \(\mathcal{N}(\mu, \mathrm{diag}(\sigma^2_{\text{fused}}))\), scores each by \(\lVert f_\theta(z_t, \mathbf{a}) - z_g \rVert_2^2\), and moves the mean to the softmax-weighted average of the \(K_{\text{elite}}\) lowest-cost candidates for \(J\) iterations, holding \(\sigma_{\text{fused}}\) fixed. The controller executes the first action and replans from the next observation.

Interactive: Product-of-Gaussians fusion

Drag the prior mean in the action plane, or use the sliders. The planner default is \(\mathcal{N}(0, 1)\) per coordinate, as in the experiments (\(\sigma_\pi = 1.0\), \(s = 1\)). The fused mean is \(w\,\mu_p\) with prior weight \(w = \tau_p / (\tau_\pi + \tau_p)\). The 64 samples use the same standard-normal draws before and after fusion.

Limitations

  • Action predictability weakens for distant goals, and inaccurate, overconfident priors can impair planning.
  • The utility of uncertainty-guided sampling depends on the task and the quality of the learned prior.
  • PoG may discard multimodal structure in the prior.
  • Real-robot results use 40 trials per condition and require broader validation.
